A tip is a gratuity for good service — any percentage shown here is a suggestion, and 0% is always allowed for poor service. Tipping norms vary by region and service type. How this is calculated

What is the formula?

Tip = bill × tip percent ÷ 100. Total = bill + tip (+ sales tax if you entered a tax rate). Per person = total ÷ party size. At $58.40 with an 18% tip: tip = $58.40 × 0.18 = $10.51, total = $68.91, and $34.46 per person for a party of 2.

Should I tip on tax?

Etiquette guides say gratuity should be calculated on the pre-tax bill — the service you received has nothing to do with the tax rate. By default this calculator follows that convention and taxes only shift the grand total. Check "Tip on tax too" if a restaurant's suggested-tip panels (which are usually computed on the taxed total) are what you want to match.

How do I count a tip mentally?

Move the bill's decimal point one place left for 10%, then double it for 20% — or for 15%, take 10% plus half of that. On a $58.40 bill: 10% is about $5.84, so 20% is about $11.68 and 15% is about $8.76. Rounding the tip up to the next dollar is the classic tidy trick this tool can do for you.

How much should I tip at a restaurant in the U.S.?

Common ranges are 15% for adequate service, 18–20% for good service, and 20–25% for excellent service — percentages are suggestions, not obligations. A flat dollar amount is fine for counter service or small checks. What matters most: tip in a way that reflects the service you got.

How does splitting the bill work for uneven orders?

This tool splits the check evenly: per person = grand total ÷ party size. That's the fastest way when everyone spent about the same. For one person's $30 steak against another's $12 salad, even splitting overcharges someone — in that case it's fairer to calculate each tip on the subtotal each person actually consumed.
