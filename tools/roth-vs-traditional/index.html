Roth vs traditional 401(k): which leaves you more after tax?

How the comparison works

Why contributions differ between the two accounts

A traditional contribution is made with pre-tax dollars, so $10,000 costs you $10,000 of salary. A Roth contribution is made with money you already paid tax on, so funding the same $10,000 of Roth space costs about $13,158 of salary at a 24% rate. To keep the comparison fair, this calculator holds the take-home cost constant and converts the Roth contribution to its after-tax equivalent.

What the break-even tax rate means

If your retirement tax rate equals your current rate, the two accounts tie (ignoring other factors). If you expect a lower rate later, the traditional account tends to win; if you expect a higher rate, Roth tends to win. Small rate changes move the result less than people expect over long horizons.

Where the answer changes

Roth is usually more valuable when you are in a low bracket now, expect rising rates, want tax diversification, or plan to leave the account to heirs. Traditional is usually more valuable when you are in a high bracket now, plan to retire into a lower bracket, or need the deduction to fund the contribution at all.

Assumptions this tool makes

Contributions are made at the end of each year, returns compound annually at a constant rate, and the existing balance is treated as pre-tax for the traditional account and after-tax for the Roth. Employer match is added to the traditional side and taxed at withdrawal. No fees are modeled.
