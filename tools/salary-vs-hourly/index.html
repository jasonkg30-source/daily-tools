How the calculation works

What the effective hourly rate really means

Effective hourly = annual pay ÷ hours you actually work in a year. A salary is fixed, so every extra unpaid hour lowers its true rate: $62,000 paid to someone working 40 h/week for 49 working weeks (3 weeks PTO) is $62,000 ÷ 1,960 h ≈ $31.63/hr — but to someone regularly working 50 h/week it is only about $25.31/hr.

How does a salary convert to an hourly wage?

The standard quick method divides by 2,080 hours (40 h × 52 weeks): a $62,000 salary is about $29.81/hr. That assumes full-time hours and no paid time off adjustment. Dividing by hours actually worked, as this calculator does, is more accurate when hours run above 40 or PTO weeks are significant.

How does overtime pay change the comparison?

Under the Fair Labor Standards Act, overtime-eligible (non-exempt) employees earn 1.5× their regular rate for hours beyond 40 in a workweek — a $26/hr worker putting in 45 h earns 26 × 45 = $1,170 in straight pay plus a 0.5 × $26 × 5 = $65 premium per week. Salaried positions are often exempt and get no premium no matter the hours, which is why unpaid salary overtime widens the gap.

Which offer should I take?

Look at both rows. If the salary pays more per year but less per hour worked, you are trading extra hours for the total — worth it only if the salary job is steadier, has better benefits, or the hours are temporary. If the hourly job pays more per hour but less per year, check whether you can pick up hours. Benefits differences often swing the decision more than 5–10% of pay does.
