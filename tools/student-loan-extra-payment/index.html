How this is calculated

The actual formula

Each month the balance grows by interest first: interest = balance × APR ÷ 12. The payment then reduces the balance. The simulation repeats until nothing is left, so extra payments shorten the term (fewer payments) and skip that many months of interest. Payoff length in years is the final payment count ÷ 12.

Why an early extra payment beats a later one

Principal removed today stops compounding for every remaining month of the loan; the same dollar paid in year 9 only stops a few months of interest. Earlier is strictly better at any positive APR. This page shows the same loan with a fixed extra from month one, so the benefit shown is the front-loaded maximum.

Prepayment penalty and the 0.25% autopay discount

Federal and most private US student loans have no prepayment penalty, so extra principal costs nothing beyond the cash itself. Many servicers apply a small rate discount for auto-debit payments; select it above and both payoff and interest reflect the discounted rate.

Assumptions this tool makes

A fixed rate for the whole life of the loan (a reasonable fit for most student loans), no fees, contributions applied at month end, and the servicer applies extra dollars to principal rather than future payments (you usually have to instruct this).
